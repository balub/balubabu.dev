CoryDora is an open-source, QMK-compatible 3×3 macropad. I designed it,
3D printed it, and sold it independently — no factory, no distributor, no
crowdfunding campaign. Just me, a printer, and a bill of materials.

And yes, it's named after the corydoras catfish. The aquarist in me was
never going to let a naming opportunity like that go.

## Why a macropad?

A macropad is close to the perfect first hardware product. It's small enough
that one person can own the entire stack — PCB, firmware, enclosure,
packaging — but real enough that every hard lesson about making physical
products shows up in miniature: sourcing, tolerances, assembly time,
shipping, support.

## The part nobody warns you about: the supply chain

The electronics were the easy part. The hard part was everything around
them — getting components reliably, at sane prices, on timelines you can
actually promise a customer. When your product depends on parts crossing an
ocean, your product is only as reliable as the most fragile link in that
chain.

That experience turned into a talk at IndiaFOSS 2025:
[CoryDora: A Macropad, A Supply Chain, and A Case for Local Manufacturing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UiJbgOzwvzU).
The short version of the argument: if we want more makers in India shipping
real hardware, we need a local manufacturing ecosystem they can lean on —
not one where every prototype depends on a fragile global supply chain.

## Going smaller: Pigmy-CoryDora

The follow-up, [Pigmy-CoryDora](https://github.com/balub/Pigmy-CoryDora),
is a compact 3-key version built around the Glyph Mini RP2040. Smaller
footprint, same personality — and a chance to apply everything the first
version taught me.

## What I'd tell you if you're thinking about it

Everything — hardware designs, firmware, and enclosure files — is open
source in the [CoryDora repo](https://github.com/balub/CoryDora). If you're
in India and thinking about making your own hardware product: the
electronics will not be your bottleneck. Plan for the boring parts, because
the boring parts are the product.

If any of this resonates, I gave an earlier talk at ChennaiFOSS on
[building open hardware with FOSS tools](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pFYOFzUogiU)
that covers the toolchain side of the story.
